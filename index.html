Privacy Policy — finance-tracking-app

Last updated: 13 November 2026

finance-tracking-app helps you see where your money goes by reading bank statements you choose to import. This policy describes exactly what the app collects, where it goes, and what it never does. If you do not agree with this policy, do not use the app.

What the app collects

Your account. You sign in with Apple or Google. We store the account identifier issued by our authentication provider and the email address that provider supplies. We never see or store your Apple or Google password.

Bank statements you import. When you import a statement, the file is uploaded to our server, read, and the transactions inside it are saved to your account.

The uploaded file itself is not kept. It is parsed in memory and discarded when the request finishes. We retain only a record about the import — the filename, which bank it came from, how many rows were read, how many were new, and any error — so the app can show you what you imported and detect duplicates.

Transactions from those statements. For each transaction we store the date, the amount and currency, the description exactly as the bank printed it, the running balance where the bank supplies one, and where present the counterparty account number, the merchant name, and the card-network category description.

Your corrections. When you assign a category to a transaction or give a counterparty a name such as "Mom" or "Rent", we store that so future transactions can use it.

What the app does not collect

  • No location data
  • No contacts, photos, or device identifiers for tracking
  • No advertising identifiers; the app contains no advertising or analytics SDKs
  • No banking credentials. The app never asks for your online banking login and has no ability to connect to your bank. Importing a file is the only way data enters the app.

What is sent to the AI service, and what is not

To work out that KFC BUSINESS PLAZA is a restaurant, the app sends merchant name text only to Anthropic's API.

It never sends:

  • amounts or balances
  • dates
  • counterparty account numbers
  • your account identifier, email, or any way to link a merchant name back to you

It is also restricted in which rows may be sent at all. Only a row that the bank itself marked as a card purchase at a merchant is eligible. Person-to-person transfers are never sent, because in Mongolia a transfer description is usually a person's name, and no automatic rule can reliably tell a person's name from a shop's name. The restriction is an allow-list: anything that does not clearly qualify is excluded.

Anthropic processes this text to return a category and does not use it to train models.

What is shared between users, and what never is

Merchant names are shared. When the app learns that a normalized merchant string such as nomin supermarket means "Groceries," that pairing is stored in a dictionary shared by all users, so nobody's app has to work it out twice. This dictionary contains only the merchant text and the category — no amounts, no dates, no user identifiers. A shop is a business, not a person.

Counterparty labels are never shared. The name you give an account number — "Mom," "Landlord" — is stored privately against your account only. An account number identifies a specific human being, so one person's label for it is never visible to anyone else. This is enforced by the database schema, not by policy alone.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

Your data is kept until you delete it.

You can delete your account from inside the app, under Settings → Delete my account. This immediately and permanently removes your account, every transaction, every account record, and every counterparty label you created. It cannot be undone and does not require contacting us.

Shared merchant-to-category pairings are not deleted, because they contain nothing personal and nothing that can be traced to you.

Security

  • All traffic between the app and the server uses HTTPS.
  • Every request must carry a valid signed token; the server verifies the signature against our authentication provider's published keys on every request.
  • The database has row-level security enabled with no access policies, so the public key shipped inside the app can read nothing. The application server is the only path to data, and it filters every query by the signed-in user.

No system is perfectly secure, and we do not claim otherwise.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their data.
